- Dependencies is something we always need in our project, is just one of those things that can't be separated to our project. Maybe you need to add a Jetpack Compose Component in your project, maybe you want to use Machine Learning library, or Math library.
- If you see the official documentation on the Android development site, you're going to see that they're using a different setup compares to yours. They don't use .toml file, they directly write to the Kotlin DSL called build.gradle.kts, and done, it was so easy, why don't we also use the same setup as them. They actually have a solid reason of why they separated it, like portability and such, but yeah why don't they update the documentation, and we can't really control our setup especially when we use the template that they gave us in the first menu of the Android Studio App, and we just want to finish our project and don't really care with these things. Honestly, I think they should make it like npm, it's so easy and convinient.
- Thankfully, it's easier than you thought, well not as easy as the original way. But it is doable, here's the secret for you **lib.version.toml** file is your best friend. You manage pretty much every dependencies in there, you configure their name, version, and package name in this file. After that, go to you gradle file of your app, and add the name of the package you just configured.
