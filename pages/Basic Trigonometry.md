- In math there is trigonometry, functions like `sine`, `cosine`, `tangent` are the most popular. They're representing a ratio of the side of a triangle.
-
- For example:
- Sin = Opposite/Hypotenuse
  logseq.order-list-type:: number
- Cos = Adjacent/Hypotenuse
  logseq.order-list-type:: number
- Tan = Opposite/Adjacent
  logseq.order-list-type:: number
-
- An easy way to remember is `SohCahToa`. Look at the uppercase latter above.
-
- Usually they also have angle in their parameter or idk what its called. Example:
- `Sin(angle) = Opposite/Adjacent`
-
- In calculator, if you put `sin(angle)` it will output the ratio of the `opposite/adjacent`.
- And if you want to do the opposite, give the ratio and output the angle, you'll need the `inverse trigonometry` functions.
-
- Because it also has to do with angle, trigonometry is also very useful for Circle too besides Triangle.
-
- There are actually three more fundamental functions: `cosecant`, `secant`, `cotangent`, they're pretty similar.
- if `sin = opposite/hypotenuse` then `csc = hypotenuse/opposite`  or `csc = 1/sin`.
-
- These pair are just switching place between nominator and denominator:
- Sine <-> Cosecant
  logseq.order-list-type:: number
- Cosine <-> Secant
  logseq.order-list-type:: number
- Tangent <-> Cotangent
  logseq.order-list-type:: number
-
- > Note that those aren't the same as inverse trigonometry functions.
-
- ## Inverse Trigonometry Functions
-
- Inverse trigonometry functions are the opposite, it takes a ratio, and gives angle as an output usually in radians.
-
- $$arcsin(opposite/hypotenuse) = angle$$
-
- Inverse `sine` is `arcsine` or simply `arcsin`, `asin`
- Inverse `cosine` is `arccosine` or simply `arccos`, `acos`
- Inverse `tangent` is `arctangent` or simply `arctan`, `atan`
- And so on. If you don't remember, just add the power of -1 to the function name, like $$sin^-1$$
